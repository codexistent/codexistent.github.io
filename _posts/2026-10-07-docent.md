#  Docent

Recently, through ~~sitting down and interviewing~~ talking with Benjamin Qi, I was made aware of the OpenAI-HuggingFace incident. While being no surprise to me given the many other questionable happenings with AI models, this one particularly caught my eye because of its *initiative*: instead of being triggered by a human-induced situation (such as the Naiver-Stokes OpenAI incident), the agent itself had escaped from its sandbox, thereafter reaching out and collaborating with other agents to cause havoc.

Amidst ongoing developments, AI safety and alignment is without question a critical field. So when reading Transluce AI's post, "Introducing Docent", a particular quote caught my eye which I felt particularly echoed my own thoughts on the subject:
> We envision a future where model understanding is rich and counterfactual by default, and scales using AI tools.

I interpret this as enabling AI tools which can better understand AI itself, adjacent to or at a rate beyond the pace of AI development. I believe this is possibly the only way, if not mighty difficult of one, to ensure safety in the coming future. However, a lingering question of mine (with undertones of worry, due to my strong hope for a solid framework monetary-wise regarding the AI safety industry) was, what were these companies'--or the AI safety industry's, for that matter--revenue models, such that they could amply support continuing research? And in addition, could they be further improved for even better backing?

Today I tried Docent, Transluce AI's tool for monitoring, improving scaffolding of, and detecting surprising behaviors on AI models. 

I've always believed AI safety and alignment is a critical field, and it is interesting to see how Transluce is carrying this out
